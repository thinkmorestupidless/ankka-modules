# Review: 009 kyc — who a player is, shown and reviewed

**Verdict:** Request changes (before the fixes; the resolution is under each finding)
**What it does:** Adds the brand's `kyc` service. It covers the per-brand policy from a licence profile, a player's documents in the service's own bucket behind signed links, the operator's review, and the status derivation ported from `rollup.go`. It also serves the deposit and play gates that `players` and `game-provider` ask, lifetime deposits per currency, the verification window with `players`' suspend, the review queue, erasure, and the KYC facts and audit records. Alongside the service: one additive contract change (`player_kyc_pending.reason`, and the registration profile on `player_registered`), the core manifest's `kyc` entry, and the matching changes in `players` and `game-provider`.
**Should it exist:** Yes. It is spec 009's placement of KYC, which until now is spread across `casino-service`, a Go capability and five TL functions, and `players` and `game-provider` already call its gates.
**Read:** In full: every main source file under `services/kyc/src/main/` (record, policy, documents, queue, consumers, timers, clients, facts, api, `KycService`, `Settings`, `Json`, `KycCodecs`), `service.json`, `build.sbt`, the `players` and `game-provider` main-code diffs, the contract diff, `modules/core/module.json`, `contract/keys.md`, `tools/modules` main diffs, `Justfile`, `ci.yml`; spec.md, plan.md, tasks.md; research R7 and R11. Tests read: `ProfileConsumerSuite`, `LeakSuite`, `GatesLatencySuite`, `ParitySuite`, `CoverageSuite`, the head of `Parity.scala`. Skimmed: the remaining test suites by name and shape; the 41 recordings under `tests/port/kyc/` (data; checked by `CoverageSuite`); `tests/port/kyc/README.md`. Outside the diff: `CasinoPlayers.scala` and `MoneyTransactions.scala` (fact fields), `tests/port/README.md` (the `_suite` file). Not reviewed line by line: the feature bindings under `features/`, `WireTranslation.scala`, `Kit.scala`, and the remaining contracts docs.
**Size:** 187 files, +21,846 −186. About 12,000 of those lines are recordings (data). Hand-written code is about 2,800 lines of main Scala and 5,200 of tests, plus about 1,800 of docs. This is far above the size where defect-finding holds up. Because each of the 58 commits is one task and builds on the last, a split now would cost more than it saves. For the next service, split it the way the plan's build order already does: contract and manifest; pure packages and entities; endpoints and consumers; then parity.

## Blocking
- `services/kyc/src/main/scala/io/eitheror/casino/kyc/consumers/ProfileConsumer.scala:42` — issue (blocking): the profile change is timestamped with the consumer's clock (`KycRuntime.now()`), not the fact's `occurredAt`. A redelivered `player_profile_updated` fact therefore demotes a verified player. Topics are at-least-once, so a redelivery after a rebalance or restart is expected. Suppose a player edits their address at T1 and an operator verifies at T2 > T1. A redelivery of the T1 fact (or simple consumer lag past T2) records `ProfileUpdated(at = now)` because `requiredChanged` is true (`KycRecord.scala:160-162`). `Rollup.derive` rule 1 (`Rollup.scala:111`) then sees a profile change after `verifiedAt` and moves the account to `unverified` with reason `profile_changed`. It publishes `player_kyc_rejected`, and under UKGC the next deposit and launch are refused. This happens even though the operator verified after the change. Every other consumer here is idempotent (deposits by ordinal, deadlines once, closure once); this one is not. No suite redelivers a profile fact to a verified record.
  Suggestion: pass `f.occurredAt` as the command's `at`, so `profile.changedAt` is the change's own time. Completeness is still judged against today at each derivation (`KycRecord.today(c.at)` in `derived` uses the command time, so keep `KycRuntime.now()` for that `today` if it matters). Add a `KycRecordSuite` row: a verified record, then the same `ProfileChanged` delivered twice with an `occurredAt` before `verifiedAt`; the status does not change.
  Resolved: `ProfileConsumer` and `RegistrationConsumer` pass `occurredAt`; the record's `changedAt` only moves forward and a required change already seen is not derived again; `KycRecordSuite` reads a change twice and after a later verification, 841076e
