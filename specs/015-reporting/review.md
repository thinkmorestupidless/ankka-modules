# Review: 015 reporting — the brand's reporting service, and the producer amendments it needs

**Verdict:** Request changes
**What it does:** Adds `services/reporting`, which folds the brand's facts into intake entities, a `BrandDay` per reporting day and views. From those it serves the original's Reports tab, the dashboard and the dispute report, plus the new day-end, compliance exports, prediction-market reports and document lifecycle. It also amends the contract and five producers (players, game-provider, wallet, merchant, psp-gateway) so that test accounts, game names, chargebacks, wagering progress and taker fees exist as facts.
**Should it exist:** yes. FR-001 forbids figures from request-time calls, and the original sampled twenty players per request. Folding facts per brand is the right layer, and putting the missing producer fields on facts (not on reads of other services' stores) follows constitution IV.
**Read:** spec.md (FRs, SCs), plan.md, tasks.md, and research.md R4, R8, R9 and *Found during implementation*. Every producer hunk in full: contract facts, wallet (`ServiceEndpoint`, `PaymentEntity`, `publish/Facts`), merchant (`CaptureRoutes`, `Endpoints`, `Payment`, `OutcomeReporter`, `WalletClient`, `AuditFacts`), psp-gateway (`DepositSession`, `Outcome`, `OutcomeDeliverer`, `Receivers`, `Webhooks`, `UnityFinance`, `SessionsView`), players (`OperatorProfileRoutes`, `PlayerEntity`, `Facts`) and game-provider (`Callbacks`, `Round`, `RoundModel`, `RoundFacts`). Skimmed, read by the identifiers the findings touch: `api/{Dashboard,Exports,Jobs,Predictions,Compliance,Wire}`, `render/DisputeReport`, the remaining `reads/*`, `intake/*` and `predictions/*`, and `Settings`. Tests read: `it/RevenueSuite` (SC-004), `it/Kit.rebuild`, and `perf/PerfSuite`'s seeding. CI, the Justfile, `build.sbt`, `service.json` and `module.json` were read. ## Blocking

- `services/reporting/src/main/scala/io/eitheror/casino/reporting/day/CloseDays.scala:50`: issue (blocking). `close-days` closes every open day once its end plus `CLOSE_GRACE` has passed, without asking whether the intake and `to-days` consumers have read that far. A day is therefore fixed with partial sections whenever the consumers are behind at the close. Three cases make this happen:
  - a new `REPORTING_GENERATION`, the designed recovery path (R8). It replays the topics from the earliest offset, and each past day is closed within five minutes of its first fold;
  - the first deployment over topics that already hold history;
  - any downtime or lag of reporting that spans midnight plus 15 minutes. At restart the timer closes yesterday before the backlog is read.

  Every fact folded after that point becomes a `Corrected`. The revenue report survives because `Revenue.daySection` adds corrections back (research, T035–T036). The day-end does not: `DayEnd.of` renders the `DayFixed` sections, so its deposits, games, GGR and chargebacks are partial. `liabilityAtClose` and `pendingHoldsAtClose` are read from today's views (`DayEndInputs.gather`). The games and differences come from a `Rounds` view that is itself still being rebuilt. The replayed facts are all dated `arrivedAt = now`, so the latest day-end's `openingCorrections` fills with the whole history. This breaks FR-018 (the day-end's figures), SC-004 (rebuild and rerun every report: identical figures) and SC-008's meaning. No suite catches it, because `RevenueSuite`'s SC-004 test compares only `/reports/ggr`.
  Suggestion: close a day only past a watermark. Each intake consumer records the latest `occurredAt` it has folded as well as the earliest. Change `Coverage` to `earliest` + `latest`, or add a sibling entity, and have the `to-days` folders record the last source time they folded. `DayClosing.run` then closes `day` only when every topic in the generation with traffic has a `latest` at or after `endOf(day) + grace`. As a backstop for a quiet topic, also accept a wall-clock bound well past the grace. Add a `DayEndSuite` case that publishes a day, closes it, rebuilds into a new generation, and asserts that `GET /day-end/{day}` is byte-identical in its sections and that today's opening holds no replayed corrections. Until a watermark exists, `/status` should not report `rebuilding: false` for a generation still catching up (research T031 records the ankka gap; the watermark above also answers it).
  Resolved: a day closes only once every to-days consumer has read past its end plus the grace, or has been idle for `CLOSE_IDLE` (15m), recorded per generation in `FoldProgress` (decided 2026-11-09, research.md); DayEndSuite rebuilds into a new generation and compares the day-end, 2f2ae2fd, f7764fa5, b0ae61b7, 11233ce9

### Blocking

- `services/reporting/src/main/scala/io/eitheror/casino/reporting/day/FoldProgress.scala:73` with `day/CloseDays.scala:84`: issue (blocking). The gate treats a consumer's `latest` (the greatest fact time it has folded) as a watermark. That only works if the consumer reads its facts in time order. In production it does not. In ankka 0.16 each `to-days` consumer reads its source's journal in 4 slices at once (`parallelism`, `docs/build/consumers.md`), spread across the service's instances. `FoldProgress.record` takes the maximum over every instance's tell. The intake consumers write those journals from topics of 6 or 12 partitions (`modules/core/module.json`), and ankka keeps no order across partitions (`docs/build/topics.md`, *Reading partitions in parallel*). So during a rebuild, a first deployment over history, or a backlog after downtime, one slice or partition can be past `end(D) + grace` while another still holds D's facts. `behind` then answers nothing and D is fixed with partial sections. That is the first round's defect, on the same three paths. The size of the skew needs a run to establish. A consumer fetching a backlog takes about one batch per partition per poll. At a brand's rate (R17: a million bets a month over 12 partitions), one partition's batch spans hours of fact time, and R17 measures a month's replay as hours long. The test's own comment (`it/DayEndSuite.scala:179-181`) sees older days of the kit close mid-replay and puts it down to the kit's facts being out of date order. It assumes "a brand's topics are in the order its facts happened". A brand's partitioned topics are not in that order across partitions, so a brand would see the same thing.
  Suggestion: count a consumer as read past D only while it is live. That means `latest ≥ end(D) + grace` and `latest` within a bound of the clock (for example `now − latest ≤ CLOSE_IDLE`); otherwise wait for idleness. During a replay, `latest` is far behind the clock, so no day closes until the replay ends. Once it ends, live traffic brings `latest` close to now, and skew between partitions is then seconds, well inside the grace. This stays within the decision of 2026-11-09 ("has folded a fact dated after the day's end plus the grace, or has folded nothing for 15 minutes") and adds one condition. A stricter alternative is a per-slice or per-partition `latest` and the minimum over them. That needs the slice or partition in `messageContext`; if ankka does not expose it, that is an ankka finding. Add a `CloseDaysSuite` case where `latest` is past the day but far behind `now`, and assert that the day waits. Correct R7 and the test comment, which state the ordering assumption.
  Resolved: a consumer counts as read past a day only while live (its latest fact within `CLOSE_GRACE` of the clock); one behind the clock waits for `CLOSE_IDLE` (decided 2026-11-09); CloseDaysSuite's four cases, the rebuild test asserts through the closer; remaining within the decision: `latest` is a maximum across slices, and per-slice progress needs ankka to expose the slice, a3c92f81
