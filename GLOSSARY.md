## Terms

### player
A person registered at one brand, with an account, wallets and personal data held under their id. A player belongs to one brand; the same person at another brand is another player.

### brand
One commercial casino: its own players, operators, wallets, facts and versions, created from one manifest and running on its own. A brand answers for itself and for no other brand.

Avoid: project, tenant

### installation
One deployment of the platform: every brand on it, the shared payments, the shared audit and the programme. A brand licensed elsewhere is a second installation.

### operator
A member of staff who runs a brand or the installation: applies manifests, adjusts balances, adds networks, sees undelivered facts, lost answers and parked sends. Not a manager, who runs the programme.

### manifest
The one description a brand is created from: its parts and their versions, the facts of the contract, what it allows the shared payments, the programme and the shared audit, and whether it takes real money.

### contract
The list of facts every brand publishes and reads, each by kind, version and the part that publishes it. The one thing two parts of a brand agree on without reading each other.

### fact
One statement that something happened, published by the part that owns it for any reader that needs it: a bet placed, a deposit confirmed, a player registered, a click. A fact has a kind, a version, an id that is the same every time it is emitted, and is about one thing so that the facts about it stay in order. A spec calls a kind of fact a family.

Avoid: notify, event, message

### view
What a reader builds for itself from the facts it reads. A reader never reads another part's store; a view can be rebuilt from the facts at any time.

### shared payments
The installation's one handling of payment providers, shared by every brand: it captures a deposit and hands it to the brand's wallet, and reports each payment's later outcome the same way. It holds a player's id and nothing else that identifies the player.

Avoid: merchant, psp-gateway

### shared audit
The installation's one record of what every brand did, kept outside the brands and naming a player by a pseudonym and never by a real id. Its registry alone maps a real id to its pseudonym, and is read only by a recorded re-identification.

Avoid: reasoner

### audit record
One entry saying who did what, when, and what changed: its actor and kind, its subject, the part, the action, the outcome and reason code, its causation and correlation, and the changes' before and after, with no value for a personal field. Written once, as the action is done, by the part that owns it, for every money command and refusal, and by the programme for every account, membership, plan, payout, adjustment and subtree action; its id derives from the part, the entity and the command's key.

### game provider
The outside system that runs the games and, for each round, places the bet, credits the win, rolls the bet back or settles the round as a loss with the wallet.

Avoid: ST8, st8

### payment provider
An outside system that moves money in or out for a player: it captures a deposit, pays a withdrawal, and reports outcomes to the brand by webhook.

Avoid: PSP, psp

### personal data
A field that identifies a person, held under that person so that it can be erased: a name, an email address, a telephone number, a postal address, a date of birth, a document, a network address, a device identifier, free text written about the person, an affiliate's payment details. Amounts, currencies, ids, times, statuses, game identifiers, a country and a pseudonym are not personal data.

Avoid: PII, personal value

### erasure
Making every field of personal data about a person unreadable, everywhere it is held, while every amount, id and time about them stays readable.

### retention hold
A hold placed on an erasure request while a regulator or finance requires the records kept; when it lapses the erasure is applied without being asked again.

### original
The platform that runs real money today, which the rebuild is held to: where a behaviour exists there, it is kept.

### recording
What the original answered to one scenario of its own end-to-end suite, run locally with every provider stubbed, kept in this repository and replayed by a parity test.

### stub
A stand-in for an outside system (a game provider, a payment provider) that answers as the real one would, so that the original can be run and recorded with no request leaving the machine.

### parity test
A test of a rebuilt part that replays one recording and fails when the part answers differently from the original, naming the recording and the field.

### spec
The description of one change to the system, naming the scenarios in the living features that hold it to account, and for every requirement what established it.

### wallet
One player's ledger in one currency: real balance, restricted funds, open rounds, deposit turnover, the pending withdrawal request, and every transaction. A player with two currencies has two wallets. Created by its first deposit or award and never deleted.

### real balance
The money in a wallet that is the player's own: deposited, won on real-funded bets, or converted from restricted funds. Only real balance can be withdrawn. Held as two pools, the deposit pool and the converted pool, which always sum to it.

Avoid: cash balance, real wallet

### restricted funds
Money a brand awarded a player that cannot be withdrawn until wagered: held as restricted funds records, drawn on by bets in priority order, converted to real balance when a record's release progress meets its wagering requirement, forfeited otherwise.

Avoid: bonus balance, bonus wallet, bonus funds, bonus money

### restricted balance
The sum of the balances of a wallet's open restricted funds records. Derived, never commanded.

### restricted funds record
One award's restricted funds in a wallet: its balance, wagering requirement, release progress, priority, open round count, the rules it was awarded under, and its state: active, awaiting its cap, forfeited or closed.

### award
The giving of restricted funds to a player, which creates a restricted funds record; an award can be forfeited, cancelled with a clawback, or made primary.

Avoid: grant, bonus grant

### wagering multiplier
The number an award's amount is multiplied by to give its wagering requirement; zero means the award converts on its first bet.

### wagering requirement
The amount a restricted funds record's release progress must reach before it converts.

Avoid: release requirement

### release progress
How much of a restricted funds record's wagering requirement has been met: advanced by each bet's contribution, reversed by a rollback, floored at zero.

Avoid: released progress, wagered progress

### contribution
What one bet adds to a restricted funds record's release progress: the contribution base (the leg or the stake, capped at the record's maximum stake) times the contribution rate (a whole percent, by game, provider, category or default).

### win allocation
The rule the primary restricted funds record sets for how a win is shared across the bet's legs: follow source (pro rata, the last restricted funds leg absorbing the rounding remainder) or all restricted (entirely to the highest-priority funding record).

### funding order
The rule the primary restricted funds record sets for which source funds a bet first: real first, restricted first, proportional or combined.

### partial split
Whether one bet may be funded partly from real balance and partly from restricted funds. With partial splits off, a bet real balance cannot cover alone is funded from restricted funds in full.

### priority
The order restricted funds records are drawn on: lowest priority first, then earliest awarded.

### primary
The first active restricted funds record in priority order: the one whose funding order and win allocation govern a bet, and the one an operator's adjustment or a provider credit lands on.

### leg
The part of one bet funded from one source: the real leg from real balance, a restricted funds leg from one record. A win follows the legs back to their sources; a rollback restores each leg to where it came from.

### wallet type
What funded a bet, as the original names it: real, bonus, split, or empty for a stake of zero. A win's wallet type mirrors its bet's.

### conversion
What happens when a restricted funds record's release progress meets its wagering requirement: its balance is credited to real balance and the record awaits its cap.

### deposit pool
*Proposed.* The part of a wallet's real balance that is the player's deposited money, operator credits, and the winnings earned by staking that money; never below zero. The only money affiliate GGR counts.

Avoid: deposit-origin balance, cash pool, real pool

### converted pool
*Proposed.* The part of a wallet's real balance that came from conversions, from bonus-side credits (a provider credit to real balance, a win's share whose record is gone), and from the winnings earned by staking that money; below zero only by a correction debit's shortfall. Earns an affiliate nothing.

Avoid: bonus pool, conversion pool

### parts
*Proposed.* Of one movement of real balance: how much of it was deposit pool money and how much converted pool money, summing to the amount moved; recorded on the transaction and carried on its fact.

Avoid: origin amounts, pool split

### pending cap
The state of a converted restricted funds record that stays open so a later withdrawal can cap what the player takes out; closed by closing the pending cap or when its remaining allowance is exhausted.

### maximum convertible amount
The most a restricted funds record's conversion lets the player withdraw; the remaining allowance starts at it.

### remaining allowance
How much of a converted record's maximum convertible amount a withdrawal may still draw on; a hold draws it down and voids the excess.

### deposits since conversion
The deposits a player made after a restricted funds record converted and before its cap was settled; a hold draws on them before the remaining allowance.

Avoid: deposits since release

### forfeit
Zeroing a restricted funds record and closing it as forfeited: by the brand's reward rules, by a hold under the record's withdrawal policy, by an operator's debit that leaves it at zero, or automatically when its balance is zero and its last open round settles as a loss.

### clawback
An amount taken from real balance when an award is cancelled or forfeited by a hold, clamped to the real balance there is.

### withdrawal policy
What a hold does to an active restricted funds record: none leaves it untouched; funds forfeits it; funds and winnings forfeits it and applies the clawback; cancel promotion forfeits it and reports the award for cancellation.

### scope
The games, providers and categories a restricted funds record's rules include or exclude; its violation mode says what a bet outside the scope does.

### violation mode
What a bet outside a restricted funds record's scope does: block refuses the bet; otherwise the record contributes nothing.

### maximum stake
The largest bet a restricted funds record's rules allow, enforced as block (the bet is refused) or as cap (the contribution base is capped).

### open round count
How many open rounds a restricted funds record has a leg in; a record at zero balance is forfeited when its count reaches zero on a loss.

### round
One play of a game, opened by a bet and closed by a win, a rollback or a loss. A round is known to one wallet by its key.

### bet
The stake a player puts on a round, funded from real balance and restricted funds by legs. The original's game provider calls it a debit.

### stake
A bet's amount.

### win
Money credited to a player for a round, following the bet's legs back to their sources. The original's game provider calls it a payout; in these features a payout is only an affiliate's.

### rollback
The undoing of a bet: each leg restored to its source, each contribution reversed, the bet voided and the round closed. The original's game provider calls it a cancel.

### loss
The settlement of a round in which the player won nothing: no money moves, the bet is marked as a loss and the round closes.

### transaction
One entry in a wallet's ledger: its type, wallet type, amount, balance before and after, currency, status, references, reason, actor and created time.

### money command
Any command that moves money in a wallet: a bet, a win, a rollback, a deposit, an award, a cancel, a hold, a release, a reversal, an adjustment, a provider credit. Every money command carries a key.

### key
The name a caller gives one money command, under which sending it again returns the first outcome and moves nothing: the game provider's transaction id, the payment reference, the request id.

Avoid: idempotency key

### deposit
Money a payment provider captured for a player, confirmed once to the wallet under its payment reference, crediting real balance and recording its required turnover.

### payment reference
The id of one payment, which is the key of its deposit.

### deposit record
What a wallet keeps about one deposit: its amount, turnover multiplier, required turnover and whether that turnover is pending or cleared.

### deposit turnover
How much a player must wager from real balance before withdrawing: each deposit's amount times its turnover multiplier. Reduced by each bet's real leg, never restored by a rollback; a hold is refused while any is pending.

### withdrawal
Money a player takes out of real balance: requested, held, then completed, cancelled, rejected, failed or reversed. Never funded from restricted funds.

Avoid: cashout

### hold
The taking of a withdrawal's amount out of real balance while the request is pending; at most one hold is pending on a wallet. The original debits the request at once; a hold is that debit, named.

### release
The return of money set aside to where it came from: a held amount to real balance when its request is cancelled, rejected or failed, clearing the pending request; or part or all of a reservation to the available amount.

### reversal
The return of a confirmed withdrawal's amount to real balance.

### adjustment
A credit or debit a person with authority enters by hand with a reason: an operator's on a wallet's real balance or restricted funds, a manager's on an affiliate's ledger. Never counted as a deposit.

Avoid: admin adjust, manual adjustment

### provider credit
A standalone amount the game provider credits outside a round: a free-spin win, a promotional payout. It lands on the primary restricted funds record if one is active, else on real balance.

### GGR
Gross gaming revenue: total stake minus total win, per player and reporting day, as signed minor units.

### terminal
A sub-brand's named way of paying through one payment provider: a scheme, regions and properties, taking deposits and, only when said so, withdrawals; the one a deposit came through is recorded on its transaction.

### affiliate
A person or company that sends players to a brand and is paid commission for what they do. One account in the programme, with a membership per brand.

### network
An issuer of tags and reader of facts: the programme, another installation's programme, or a third party. A brand knows a network by its id, its key document, its revocation list and the pseudonym key it holds for it.

### programme
The installation's own affiliate network, shared by every brand: it issues tags, publishes its key document, revocation list and legacy tag document, reads every brand's facts, turns them into commission and pays affiliates. It knows a player only by a pseudonym.

Avoid: affiliate service, affiliate-service

### tag
The value an affiliate's link or bonus code carries to a brand, signed by the network that issued it, naming the network, the affiliate, the link, a campaign, up to five sub-ids, the signing key and when it was issued. A brand verifies it with the network's key document and asks the network nothing.

Avoid: btag, token

### link
An affiliate's tracking link to a brand, carrying a tag; clicks and registrations are counted against it.

Avoid: tracking link

### campaign
An optional name an affiliate gives a link, carried in its tag and on every fact about a player it bound.

### CRM campaign
A CRM's automated sequence that acts on a player through the brand's CRM webhook: it awards an offer or writes an inbox item, and is answered with the outcome.

### sub-id
One of up to five values an affiliate puts in a tag to tell their own traffic apart, carried on every fact about a player the tag bound.

Avoid: subid

### click
One landing of a visitor on a brand with a valid tag, at most one per link per browser session: the tag's fields, the click id, the device type, the country and a digest of the visitor's address, never the address.

### click id
The id of one click: the one the link brought, or one the brand assigned when the link brought none. Carried from the click to the binding and into every fact about the player.

Avoid: clickId

### attribution window
How long after a tag was issued it may still bind a player at registration. A brand setting with an installation default.

### bonus code window
How long after an organic registration an affiliate's bonus code may still bind the player, counted from registration. A brand setting with an installation default.

### binding
A player's attribution to a network, affiliate, link, campaign and sub-ids, with the click id and the pseudonym, made once at the first of a valid tag or an affiliate's bonus code, and never changed.

### organic
A player bound to no affiliate: registered with no tag, a stale tag, a refused tag or a revoked link.

### later touch
A tag, bonus code or link presented for a player already bound: recorded on the attribution record with its time and changing nothing.

### attribution record
What a brand keeps about one player's attribution: the binding, the refused or stale tag when there was one, and the later touches. Part of the brand's regulated books; nothing a network says changes it.

### attributed
Of a player: bound to an affiliate, so that every fact about them names that affiliate. Of a fact: naming the affiliate the player is bound to.

### unattributed
Of a fact the programme received: naming a player it has no attribution for at that brand, a revoked link, or an affiliate with no membership there; recorded with its reason for a manager to see and writing no commission.

### pseudonym
The only name of a player a network ever sees: a keyed digest of the player's id under the brand's pseudonym key for that network, stable for the player's life, different at every brand and every network, and surviving erasure.

Avoid: hashed id, pseudonymous id

### pseudonym key
The secret a brand holds for one network, under which it derives its players' pseudonyms for that network; never rotated and never leaving the brand.

### signing key
A key under which a network signs tags, or a brand signs feed pages and network webhooks; published by id in a key document and withdrawn from it when retired.

### key document
What a network or a brand publishes listing its current signing keys by id and how long a reader may cache it.

### revocation list
What a network publishes naming the links and affiliates whose tags bind no new player; a brand refreshes it on the stated interval.

### legacy tag
A tag from before this protocol: a bare id with no network, sub-ids or signature, resolved through the programme's legacy tag document during the transition period and refused after it.

Avoid: bare UUID

### legacy tag document
What the programme publishes during the transition period mapping each imported legacy tag to its affiliate and link; withdrawn when the period ends.

### transition period
The time from a brand's first day on this protocol during which a legacy tag is still accepted.

### reverse channel
The whole of what a network may tell a brand: its key document and its revocation list. Nothing else is accepted; nothing a network says changes a binding, a fact or an amount.

### carrier
One way a reader receives a brand's facts: the programme's own reading when the brand allows it, a machine reading with its own reader, the feed, or a network webhook. Every carrier delivers at least once; a reader discards a repeat by its fact id.

### feed
The brand's facts for one network served in the order they were emitted, from a cursor, in signed pages, to a machine the installation registered.

### cursor
An opaque position in a brand's feed; reading from the same cursor twice returns the same page.

### page cursor
An opaque position in a partner's paging of a collection; one that is malformed, expired or another partner's is refused and leaks nothing.

### webhook
A payment provider's post of a payment's outcome to the gateway, verified by its signature or, for a provider that does not sign, by the secret in its address; acknowledged even when it moves nothing.

### network webhook
A network's address and shared secret a brand's operator created, to which the brand posts each fact signed, retrying with backoff for a day before keeping the fact as an undelivered fact.

### CRM webhook
The brand's address a CRM campaign posts an action to, signed under the brand's signing key and refused outside its time window.

### undelivered fact
A fact a network webhook failed to receive for a day, kept by the brand for the operator to see, resend or discard.

### lost answer
A request the cashier accepted whose answer could not be returned, kept by the shared payments with what the cashier made, for the operator to see and mark seen.

### parked send
A send the CRM refused after retries, parked by the brand with the last answer, for the operator to retry or discard; it is no longer parked once the CRM accepts it.

### machine
A network's own system registered with the installation, admitted to a brand's facts and nothing else of the brand's.

### protocol
The agreement between a brand and any network: the tag, the facts, the pseudonym, the carriers and the reverse channel. Facts carry its version; a change to a fact's shape is a new version emitted beside the old.

### bonus code
A code an affiliate hands out instead of a link; a player who presents it is bound to the affiliate as a tag would bind them. Unique per brand regardless of case.

Avoid: promo code, promotion code

### bonus cost
What a brand spent on a player's rewards: emitted as a fact when restricted funds are awarded, forfeited or converted, and folded into NGR by the programme.

### chargeback
A deposit the player's payment provider took back; emitted as a fact and folded into NGR.

### reward
One player's taking of an offer: pending on a selection until the confirming deposit, then active once awarded as restricted funds or created as free rounds at the game provider; then converted pending cap, completed, cancelled, forfeited, revoked, expired or voided. Every transition is a fact.

Avoid: grant, bonus grant, bonus instance

### round stream
A brand setting per network that emits a fact for every settled round beside the daily GGR fact.

### reporting day
One day in the brand's reporting zone; when it closes, one daily GGR fact is emitted per attributed player with activity.

### reopen
An operator's return of a closed account to active, on the player's request and within the retention hold; a reopening is not a reinstatement, which lifts a suspension or an exclusion.

### self-exclusion
A player's bar from a brand: permanent when the player sets it in one confirmed action, for at least 183 days or permanent when an operator sets it; reinstated by an operator only after the period, never when permanent. Emitted to the programme as a closed player with the category self-excluded.

### account
One affiliate's standing in the programme: identity and payment fields as personal data, a parent and an override, and a membership per brand.

Avoid: affiliate registration

### membership
An account's standing at one brand: pending, active, suspended or rejected, with the plan at that brand and the terms accepted for it.

### application
An affiliate's request to join a brand, which creates the account when the email is new and otherwise adds a pending membership; refused when the email already has a pending or active membership at that brand.

### manager
A member of staff who runs the programme for the brands their roles cover: approves applications, manages plans and settings, closes periods, reviews fraud flags.

### plan
A brand's commission terms a membership is on: CPA, RevShare or hybrid, with the CPA amount and currency, the RevShare percentage, the minimum deposit for CPA and whether negative NGR carries over. A brand's settings name the default plan for new memberships.

### CPA
Commission paid once for a player's first deposit at or above the plan's minimum deposit, in the plan's currency.

### RevShare
Commission paid as a percentage of a player's cumulative NGR, written as the delta between the commission due and what has already been emitted.

### hybrid plan
A plan that pays both CPA and RevShare.

### NGR
Net gaming revenue: GGR minus bonus cost, chargebacks and the brand's admin, payment and provider fee percentages.

### negative carryover
Whether a period's negative NGR persists as a deficit into the next period, or the period's RevShare floors at zero and the next starts fresh.

### first-deposit guard
The programme's record that CPA was paid for a player at a brand, set once for the player's life and never by a first deposit below the plan's minimum deposit.

### commission
What an affiliate is owed for the players they sent: CPA, RevShare, override and adjustments, as payable facts in a ledger.

### ledger
The immutable facts for one affiliate at one brand in one currency: NGR inputs and payable facts, each never changed except to record the payout that claimed it.

Avoid: commission account

### payable fact
A ledger fact that is owed to an affiliate: CPA, RevShare, override or an adjustment; claimed once by a payout when a period closes.

### fold
The ledger computed fresh from its facts, every time, so late and out-of-order facts correct themselves and nothing is counted twice. Every read of revenue is a fold.

### revenue
What the portal shows an affiliate or a manager: the fold of a ledger over a period, per brand and per currency, never summed across currencies.

### period
The span a brand's payout cycle closes at once: a month, a month range, a day range in the brand's reporting zone, or all time.

### close
A manager's closing of a period for a brand: every unclaimed payable fact up to the period's end is claimed by a pending payout per affiliate and currency. Repeating a close claims nothing twice.

### payout
What one affiliate is paid for one period at one brand in one currency: pending, approved, rejected or paid; reverted to pending when a later close changes its total; never changed once paid. In these features a player's money out is a withdrawal, never a payout.

### override
A parent's percentage of what each affiliate below them earns, written as an override fact per ancestor and source, recomputed as a delta when the source's earnings change.

### parent
The affiliate above another in the subtree: invites subs, approves, suspends and reactivates them, changes their plan and override, and earns override on them.

### sub
An affiliate below a parent in the subtree, created pending under the parent by an invitation.

Avoid: child affiliate

### subtree
An affiliate and every affiliate below them; what an affiliate sees and acts within, and nothing above or beside.

Avoid: network tree, downline

### invitation
A parent's one-use offer to a sub, by email or invite link, which creates the sub pending under the parent on the parent's plan unless it says otherwise.

### operator invitation
The shared payments' one-use offer to a new operator, who accepts it and sets a password to become active at their level.

### fraud flag
A warning the programme raises once per player, brand and reason when a brand's bonus cost or chargeback thresholds are crossed, for a manager to review and dismiss.

### programme page
A brand's public terms, contact, payout cycle and description that applicants see.

### portal
Where an affiliate signs in to see their memberships, revenue, breakdown, payouts, players, links, bonus codes, subtree and notifications, for one brand or for all.

### notification
A message to an affiliate in the portal: application received, approved or rejected, payout paid, a sub pending approval, a fraud flag dismissed, an invitation.

### postback
A call the programme makes to an affiliate's own address when a registration, first deposit, deposit or commission fact occurs for their player, with the kind, click id, payout amount and status substituted; retried with backoff for a day.

Avoid: callback

### launch ticket
The one-use value the brand issues when a player launches a game and the game provider presents back on its first provider call, naming the player, the game, the session, the currency and any free rounds attached; once checked it is marked used and becomes the session ticket.

Avoid: launch token

### session ticket
What a launch ticket becomes once the game provider has checked it: the value the provider carries on every provider call for the rest of the game session, from which the brand resolves the player, the wallet and its currency.

Avoid: wallet token

### game session
One player's sitting at one game, opened by the brand's player records at launch and ended by the frontend; its wagered and won totals are derived from the settled rounds, never written on it.

### provider call
One call the game provider makes to the brand during play: a check of a launch ticket, a balance read, a player profile read, a debit, a credit, a cancel or a buyin; signed by the provider over its content, keyed by the provider's transaction id where it moves money, and answered with a word and the balance even when refused.

### transaction id
The id the game provider gives one provider call that moves money, under which the brand keys the wallet's bet, win or provider credit and from which it finds the round; a repeat under the same id is answered as the first was.

### cancel id
The id the game provider gives a cancel, naming the transaction it undoes; a cancelled credit is debited back as a bet keyed by it.

### conflicting repeat
A repeated provider call under a transaction id already answered whose content differs from the first; the first outcome is answered and the difference recorded.

### unacknowledged call
A provider call that failed to reach the wallet and was answered "error", kept so that the provider's retry is served afresh; cleared when the retry succeeds, when an operator replays it against the wallet under its own transaction id, or when an operator resolves it with a reason because the money was settled another way.

### fun mode
A launch played for no money: the game provider is told so and no free rounds are attached.

Avoid: demo mode

### free rounds
A reward of a counted number of rounds on named games, created at the game provider under the reward's id, played with no stake from the wallet, and settled by the reward rules from the settled round's win. Attached to a launch ticket when eligible; never paid by the game provider's own credit.

Avoid: free spins, free-rounds grant, free spin grant

### spin value
What one free round costs at the game provider for one game and one currency, priced by the provider and cached for the day.

### buyin
A stake the game provider debits outside any round, for a tournament or a table: a bet keyed by its transaction id.

### player profile
What the game provider reads about a player before creating free rounds: their id, the brand's jurisdiction, their default currency, registration country and date, date of birth, and a bet limits band from their daily deposit limit.

### catalogue
The brand's list of games: fed nightly from the game provider's list, with each game's code, name, developer, category, labels, themes, game features, bonus types, territories, deprecation date and enabled flag; curated by the operator, who disables developers, categories or single games or enables a game the provider disabled; browsed by players.

Avoid: game list

### developer
The studio that made a game, as the game provider names it; a brand may disable every game of a developer.

### credential
What a brand or a sub-brand holds to act with an outside system on its own behalf: the game provider's, a payment provider's. Held in the brand's own secret store, never in a shared part.

### play gate
The brand's rule that a player must have reached a KYC status before they may launch a game; a launch refused by it names the player's KYC status.

Avoid: KYC gate

### KYC
The brand's knowing of who a player is: the identity, address and other checks the brand requires, each at a status, deciding what the player may do.

Avoid: know your customer

### cooling off
The state of a player on a break: what a sign-in, a launch, a bet or a deposit is refused as, naming the end of the break.

### session limit
The most minutes a player may play at a brand in a day; a launch at the limit is refused naming it.

### responsible gambling
The brand's protections for a player against their own play: deposit, loss and wager limits, the session limit, breaks, cooling off, self-exclusion and the national register. A bet the wallet refuses for one of them is answered to the game provider as a spending limit.

Avoid: RG, safer gambling

### holding
The top of the shared payments' hierarchy: a group that owns companies, under which sub-brands sit. An operator at a holding's level reaches everything below it.

### company
A member of a holding in the shared payments' hierarchy, owning sub-brands.

### sub-brand
One customer of the shared payments: the unit that holds the credentials, terminals, routing tree and cashier keys its deposits run under. A brand's casino is a sub-brand naming its brand; a sub-brand of type payments names none and its deposits credit no wallet.

Avoid: merchant id, external merchant

### cashier
The part of the shared payments that takes a deposit from a cashier session to the brand's wallet: it opens the session, has the gateway initiate the payment, keeps the payment until its outcome, hands a captured deposit to the wallet once, and reports every later outcome to the brand the same way.

### cashier key
The credential a sub-brand generates for its frontend, under which the shared payments admits a cashier session's creation, submit and read; shown raw once, listed by id and name after, and refused once disabled.

Avoid: merchant key, API key

### cashier session
What a sub-brand opens for one deposit: the amount, currency, player, country and request id, with the terminals the routing tree allows; submitted once naming a terminal, which creates the payment.

Avoid: checkout session

### cashier ticket
The value a cashier session is answered with, which the player's browser alone holds and presents to submit or abandon the session.

Avoid: session token

### request id
The id a sub-brand gives one request to the shared payments, under which a repeat with the same content returns the first answer and a repeat with different content is refused; the sub-brand's, not the installation's.

### payment
One deposit through the shared payments: created by a cashier session's submit, awaiting the player at the payment provider, then captured, failed, expired or abandoned; keyed by its payment id, under which it is confirmed to the brand's wallet.

Avoid: cashier payment, payment attempt

### payment id
The id of one payment, the key under which the cashier confirms its deposit to the wallet and reports its outcomes.

### gateway
The part of the shared payments that speaks to the payment providers: it initiates a deposit or a withdrawal payment with the provider under the sub-brand's credential, verifies the provider's webhook, and delivers each outcome to the part that asked, once.

### provider session
The gateway's record of one deposit or withdrawal payment at one payment provider, under the cashier's payment id for a deposit or the withdrawal's request id: the sub-brand, the terminal, where to deliver the outcome, and whether it was delivered.

### undelivered outcome
An outcome the gateway could not deliver to the part that asked within its retries, kept on the provider session and listable, and re-driven every two minutes until it is delivered or refused; an operator may re-drive it at once. One the receiver refused is kept and listable too, and only an operator re-drives it.

### routing tree
A sub-brand's, company's or holding's versioned rules deciding which terminals a deposit may use for its amount, currency, country and deposit count: saved as a draft, deployed as a numbered version, rolled back to an earlier one; a sub-brand with none takes its company's, then its holding's.

Avoid: routing table, routing rules

### withdrawal payment
The paying of an approved withdrawal to the player through a payment provider, initiated by the brand under the withdrawal request's id; completed, failed or reversed by the provider.

### operator level
Where in the hierarchy an operator of the shared payments acts: the installation, a holding, a company or a sub-brand, with everything below it; an operator is refused above or beside their level.

Avoid: scope level, scope violation

### rate limit
The most requests a sub-brand may send the shared payments in a minute, set for the installation and raised for one sub-brand by an operator; the excess is refused as too many with the time to wait.

Avoid: throttle

### quote
What a crypto payment provider answers for a deposit: a payment address, a coin amount, a coin currency and a deadline; a confirmation short of it fails the payment with the variance.

### withdrawal request
A player's request for a withdrawal: the amount, the terminal and the destination or crypto address, held on the wallet when created; pending review, then approved, awaiting manual payment, needs review, completed, failed, rejected, cancelled or reversed.

Avoid: cashout request, payout request

### destination
A bank account a player saved in their own name to withdraw to; one may be the default; a destination that is another player's or deleted is invalid.

Avoid: payout destination, bank destination

### queue
The brand's list of withdrawal requests for an operator to review: each with the player's identity status, amount, terminal, destination and age, filtered by status and by the withdrawal payment's status.

### in doubt
The state of a withdrawal payment the gateway or the provider has not finally answered: initiated with no outcome yet, unreachable, or unrecognised; reconciled by asking the provider's status, never offered for manual payment.

### needs review
The state of an approved withdrawal request whose withdrawal payment cannot be resolved by asking the provider: an operator confirms the payment or marks it failed.

### manual payment
An approved withdrawal paid by an operator outside the gateway, after the gateway rejected it at initiation or because the terminal has no automated withdrawal payment; the operator confirms it or marks it failed.

Avoid: manual payout

### realm
The outside system that holds the passwords and sessions of a brand's players and operators: it checks a password, asks the brand whether the player may sign in before issuing anything, issues and refreshes the sign-in, and tells the brand when a session ends.

Avoid: Keycloak, identity provider

### sign-in
A player's or operator's standing once the realm has accepted their password and the brand has said they may: carried on every request, refreshed while the session lives, revoked when it ends.

Avoid: access token, refresh token, JWT

### player session
The span from a player's sign-in to their sign-out, bound to the realm's session, recording the network address and the user agent; every game session is opened under it and ends with it. Ended by a sign-out, by the realm's session going idle, or by a suspension, exclusion or closure.

### player status
Where a player stands at the brand: pending email verification, active, suspended, self-excluded, blocked, closed or erasure pending. A restricted status refuses the sign-in and ends every session.

Avoid: account status, account state

### profile
A player's own details: given and family names, display name, date of birth, telephone, address, country, language, the fields their jurisdiction's banking needs, and the email addresses they keep, one of which is the sign-in address. Some fields lock once set.

### sign-in address
The one of a player's verified email addresses the realm signs them in with; a player makes another verified address the sign-in address and the first no longer is.

Avoid: primary email

### consent
A player's yes to marketing by one channel, recorded with the time it was given and its source, and read as withdrawn with the time from when the player withdraws it.

### cookie decision
What a player's device recorded about analytics and marketing cookies, with its version and time, kept per device; refused when a field is missing.

### device fingerprint
What the site sent to tell a player's device apart, kept as personal data under the player and compared at registration for duplicates.

### tier
The standing an operator gives a player, such as "gold"; a change carries the previous tier and names the operator.

Avoid: VIP level

### note
Free text an operator writes about a player: personal data under the player, pinned or not, never visible to the player.

### fraud case
What opens when two players of a brand match by name and date of birth or by device fingerprint: it links them, flags each as a suspected duplicate, and is reviewed or dismissed by an operator; dismissing it clears every flag it set.

### suspected duplicate
The flag a fraud case sets on each player it links; the brand's rewards withhold a reward from a flagged player.

### signal
What a fraud case was opened on: identity, for a match by name and date of birth, or device, for a match by device fingerprint.

### erasure request
The filing of a player's erasure, once in the brand and once in the shared payments under one correlation id, each with the brand's retention hold; applied by every part when the hold lapses.

### erasure response
What the brand assembles when every part has applied an erasure request: the certificates each part returned under the request's correlation id.

Avoid: data-subject response, DSAR

### document
A file a player uploaded for one KYC category: identity front, identity back, proof of address, payment method, bank account details or other; pending review until an operator approves it or rejects it with a reason code. A category's latest document is the one that counts, except in the category other, where every document is its own.

### reason code
Why a document was rejected, one of five codes rendered in the player's language, never free text: document unclear, address mismatch, document expired, invalid document, other.

### KYC status
Where a player's KYC stands: unverified, pending, additional verification required, verified or rejected; derived from the documents and the profile in automatic mode, set by the operator alone in manual mode.

### KYC policy
The brand's KYC rules: the deposit gate, the play gate, the deposit threshold, the source of funds threshold, the verification window, the required categories, and whether the window's expiry suspends; taken from a licence profile and marked custom once a field is changed by hand.

### licence profile
A regulator's defaults for the KYC policy, applied by name: UKGC, MGA or Curaçao; custom once any field is changed.

### deposit gate
The brand's rule that a player must have reached a KYC status before they may deposit; a deposit refused by it tells the player the status's own words.

### deposit threshold
The lifetime deposits above which an unverified player may deposit no more and is flagged for review.

### source of funds threshold
The lifetime deposits above which a player whose source of funds is not cleared may deposit no more and is flagged for review.

### lifetime deposits
The sum of a player's confirmed deposits over their life at the brand, in minor units of the brand's currency, which the gates compare with the thresholds.

### verification window
The days a player has from registration to become verified under the KYC policy; when it lapses unverified the player is suspended if the policy says so, else flagged for review.

### automatic mode
The KYC mode in which the KYC status follows the documents and the profile by the rules.

### manual mode
The KYC mode in which nothing changes the KYC status but the operator's own decision; entered when a category is marked required for one player.

### flagged for review
The mark put on a player for an operator to look at, with its reason: threshold, source of funds or window; raised once per reason.

### document store
Where a brand keeps documents, under each player's own prefix, encrypted at rest, readable only through the addresses the brand issues for a limited time.

Avoid: bucket, object store

### deposit limit
A player's cap on what they may deposit in a day, a week or a month, in their wallet's currency: a deposit that would pass it is refused before it is credited, naming what remains. Lowering one applies at once; raising or removing one is a pending change.

### loss limit
A player's cap on their losses over a week, shown with the losses of the period; no bet is refused for it.

### pending change
A raise or removal of a limit the player asked for, which applies 24 hours later unless they tighten the limit first or an operator sets it; a new raise replaces it and restarts the day.

### break
A player's bar from sign-in, play and deposits until a date they chose, which they cannot shorten and only an operator may lift; it ends by itself when its end passes. While it stands the player is cooling off.

Avoid: cool-off, time-out, timeout

### case
The record of one break or exclusion: its type, its trigger (player, operator, third party or system), its end if any, and how it closed (expired, lifted or closed); exported for the regulator; a note an operator wrote on it is personal data.

Avoid: RG case

### reality check
A player's chosen interval of 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes at which the game pauses until they acknowledge it; the acknowledgement is recorded with the game session. Opt-in: with none set no check fires.

### national register
The regulator's register of people excluded from every operator; a refusal it causes at sign-in is recorded as a self-exclusion case with a third party trigger.

Avoid: GAMSTOP

### offer
What an operator creates for players to claim: its type (deposit match or free rounds), percentage, maximum value, wagering multiplier, minimum deposit, deposit number, claim window, deposit window, wagering window, cap window, offer codes, validity, segment, tier floor and order; and where free rounds' winnings go, real balance or restricted funds.

Avoid: promotion, bonus offer

### selection
A player's claim of an offer that requires a deposit: the reward is pending until the next confirming deposit at or above the minimum arrives within the deposit window, and expired after it.

### offer code
A code an offer carries, entered by a player to make the offer visible and select it, matched regardless of case; a brand's own code, as against an affiliate's bonus code.

Avoid: brand code, promo code

### bonus code document
What the programme publishes for a brand mapping each affiliate's bonus code to the offer it names; a brand refuses an offer code the document already holds.

### claim window
How long after registration an offer may be claimed; past it a claim is refused.

### deposit window
How long a selection waits for its confirming deposit; past it the reward is expired.

### wagering window
The days a reward's restricted funds may be wagered before the reward expires.

### cap window
The days a converted reward waits for its cap before the pending cap is closed.

### activation baseline
The real balance recorded when a reward activated, from which a player's own cancellation computes the clawback: real winnings since activation less deposits since, floored at zero; with no baseline the clawback is zero.

### segment
A set of players the brand's engagement names by rule, which an offer or a send may be limited to.

### CRM
The outside marketing system a brand connects: it holds a profile per player that the brand identifies from its facts, receives the brand's tracks, sends the brand's transactional sends, and runs CRM campaigns that act on a player through the CRM webhook.

Avoid: Customer.io, customerio, marketing platform

### identify
The refreshing of the CRM's profile of a player with the brand's attributes, from facts and never from a read of another part's store; made before every track, and again with a zero daily deposit count when the deposit day rolls over.

### track
One telling of the CRM that something happened to a player, named by its kind and carrying its data, made once per fact after an identify and recorded in a send record.

Avoid: CRM event, tracked event

### send record
What the brand keeps about each send it made or refused for a player: the fact id, the track, the class, the channel, the time and the outcome, accepted, skipped stale, no configuration, marketing suppressed, account closed or no address.

### transactional send
An email or an inbox item the brand sends a player for one trigger, of the marketing class or the service class, under the brand's transactional id for the trigger or the built-in fallback template; keyed by the request so a repeat sends nothing.

### marketing class
The class of a send the brand may withhold: refused for a player with an open case, withdrawn consent or a closed account.

### service class
The class of a send a player must get whatever their standing: a withdrawal's outcome, a verification, the exclusion or closure confirmation.

### staleness bound
How old a fact may be at first sight before the brand's engagement records it as stale and sends nothing for it.

### deposit day
The brand's day for counting deposits, with its boundary at three in the morning; when it rolls over every player with a daily deposit count above zero is identified again with zero.

### inbox item
What a player sees in their inbox: written by the brand's player records for an operator's send or a CRM campaign's send, of the marketing or the service class; created, delivered and read are each a fact.

Avoid: in-app message, player message

### push connection
An open connection a player's page holds to the brand, under the player's sign-in, down which the brand pushes that the inbox changed; kept alive by a heartbeat, re-checked every thirty seconds, and closed with a reason.

### push
A content-free telling of a player's open page that its inbox changed, sent down every push connection the brand holds for the player on any instance; the page then fetches the inbox again.

### presence
Which players hold an open push connection, and since when; what the operator's online filter reads.

### seed person
The CRM person the brand sends one identify and one track per trigger to, so that the CRM campaign builder lists every track before any player has caused one.

### reporting zone
The time zone a brand's reporting days are counted in; a day is the calendar day in it, and a bet at half past eleven at night counts on that day.

### revenue report
Finance's report for a period in one currency: total stakes, wins, GGR, bonus cost, NGR, deposits, withdrawals and net cash flow, exact over every player, one row per reporting day, exportable.

### player report
Compliance's report of every player registered in a period with their country, statuses, lifetime figures and last sign-in, filterable, sortable and exportable in full.

### transaction report
A support agent's list of a player's transactions over a period, newest first and paged, filtered by type or searched by part of an email address, exportable in full.

Avoid: transaction ledger

### bonus report
Marketing's report for a period: per offer and in total, rewards, granted value, conversions and their rate, forfeits, cancellations and active rewards.

### provider report
Operations' report for a period: per payment provider and in total, deposit attempts, completions, value and success rate, and withdrawal requests, completions and the average hours to completion; a rate is absent when there were no attempts.

### dispute report
The document a support agent generates about a player's play for one player session or a date range of up to a year: the sessions, each game session's rounds with their figures, the totals, and a reference number; served for a day, with its record kept.

### reference number
The unique number a generated document or export carries, quoted to a player or a regulator, kept in the record of the report after the document is gone.

### job
A report or export too large to generate at once: returned with its reference number, its progress readable, its document served once ready.

### day-end
The figures fixed when a reporting day closes: per currency, deposits and withdrawals per payment provider, GGR per game provider and game beside the wallet's, bonus cost, chargebacks, the liabilities at close, and the differences between the game provider's rounds and the wallet's; a late fact is a correction carried into the next day-end, never a changed day-end.

### compliance export
An export for a regulator of the brand's cases or KYC decisions over a period, one row each, with a reference number; served for a day, with its record kept.

### test account
A player flagged as the brand's own test account, counted in every figure as the original counts it and shown with its flag.

### partner
An outside company a brand lets read its records under a partner entitlement: it signs in with a client id and secret for one brand and queries that brand's players, transactions, bets, rounds, sessions and rewards, and nothing of any other brand. It can write nothing.

### partner entitlement
What an operator creates for a partner at one brand: enabled or not, the identifying fields the partner may read, the agreement that permits them, the requests per minute and the cost ceiling; a change takes effect within a minute and is recorded with who made it.

Avoid: partner grant

### partner contract
The published description of what a partner may query, complete enough to generate a client from; byte-identical across internal changes, and a new version beside the old for at least six months when a change breaks it.

Avoid: schema, GraphQL schema

### identifying field
A field of personal data a partner may be entitled to read under its partner entitlement when an agreement is recorded; absent from every answer, not empty, when not entitled or when the player is erased.

### subject access request
A player's ask of what was read about them: every partner that was served an identifying field of theirs, with the fields and the times.

Avoid: SAR

### freshness
What a partner reads to tell how far a brand's views lag its facts: the time between the newest fact published and the newest applied, and whether the views are degraded, rebuilding, catching up or missing history; a degraded answer is provisional.

Avoid: read model status, readModelStatus

### support agent
Motoko at a brand: the agent a signed-in player chats with, which reads the player's account from the owning parts as itself, proposes actions the player must approve, and hands anything else to the support chat. Off for a brand until an operator turns it on.

Avoid: chatbot, assistant, bot

### conversation
A player's chat with the support agent under one session id: durable across restarts and pages, compacted when long, kept for the brand's retention, and encrypted under the player's key so that an erasure makes it unreadable.

### turn
One thing the player writes to the support agent and the reply it streams back; a turn whose tool needs approval ends with an approval request, and a new turn while one awaits is refused.

Avoid: chat message

### tool
One read or action the support agent may make of a part of the brand as itself: a read runs at once, an action runs once after the player's approval and takes the player from the sign-in, never from the model.

### approval request
What the support agent shows when a tool would act for the player: the exact effect in the player's words, awaiting the player's one decision to approve or cancel; decided once, and expired with the player session.

### guardrail
A check on a turn before the model sees it, or on a reply before the player does: by rule, or judged by a provider; a rejection names the guardrail, runs no tool and is audited.

### support chat
The brand's human support, with its hours, which the support agent names whenever it cannot help.

Avoid: Chatwoot

### prompt
The instructions and hand-off wording an operator sets for the brand's support agent as a numbered version; the next conversation takes the new version and conversations in progress keep theirs.

Avoid: system message

### registry
The shared audit's one store mapping a real id to its pseudonym, per brand and per kind, player, operator, affiliate or service; written when a pseudonym is first issued, read only by a recorded re-identification, and unchanged by a player's erasure.

### re-identification
A compliance officer with the re-identification role asking the registry who a pseudonym is, with a reason code, and being told the brand, the kind and the real id; recorded, as is a refusal.

### installation record
An audit record of class installation about a brand: a deployment, a rollback, a restore, a grant's change or an erasure request's state, listed beside the brand's own records in one order of occurrence.

### migration
The move of a brand from the original to the rebuild: a freeze, an export, an import, a parity report, a cutover within a window, sign-off, and the original's retirement; rehearsed against a production snapshot before the real window.

### freeze
The moment the original stops taking writes for the migration, behind a maintenance page; the export refuses to start while the wallet reports open bets.

### cutover
The switching of a brand's addresses from the original to the rebuild once the parity report is clean.

### cutover window
The span after the cutover during which the original is untouched and the operator may still go back; when it closes after sign-off the original is retired and going back is a restore.

### going back
The operator's return of a brand to the original within the cutover window: the addresses returned, the original's data unchanged since the freeze, the new brand left for study, and the players' actions on the rebuild since the switch listed for the operator.

Avoid: migration rollback

### parity report
One row per player comparing what the original answered at the freeze with what the rebuild answers after the import: OK, an explained class with its evidence named, or UNEXPLAINED, which blocks the cutover.

### retained journal
The copy of the original's journal kept after the cutover for the regulator's retention period: read only by two people with a stated reason and a record in the shared audit, erased per player by the original's erasure, and deleted as a whole when the retention lapses.

### rehearsal
A run of the migration's runbook end to end against a production snapshot in a rehearsal installation, with every step timed and every finding listed; the real cutover window is refused while a finding is open, and the production personal data is removed when the rehearsal ends.

### simulator
The stand-in game provider the installation runs for parity and failure tests: it speaks the game provider's protocol, is scripted by simulator scenarios, keeps a simulator run per session, provisions free rounds and lists fake games.

### simulator scenario
A named script the simulator follows for a session or a brand: what to send, when, and what the brand must answer; the default plays a round as the original's stub does, the others misbehave on purpose.

### simulator run
The simulator's record of one session's play: each provider call sent, the key it was signed with, the times, the answer, the attempt and the scenario step; its verdict, passed or failed with the first failing step; exportable as a recording.

### load profile
A number of sessions the simulator plays at a rate of rounds a minute for a duration against a brand, reporting rounds sent, answers by word and the percentile answer times; stopped, it settles its open rounds as losses.

### tester
The person who drives the simulator: selects a scenario, instructs a bet, a win, a loss or a cancel, spends a free round, reads a simulator run and starts a load profile.

## Modules

### module
*Proposed.* A named set of services, with the grants, topics, dependencies, configuration keys and retention its manifest declares, that a brand runs when entitled to it: core, casino, support, predictions, pool.

### core
*Proposed.* The module every brand runs: wallet, players, kyc, responsible-gambling, payouts, engagement, reporting, attribution and the vendor's agent.

### vendor
*Proposed.* eitheror as the operator of ankka-vendor: the product that releases modules, holds entitlements and enables modules for brands.

### eitheror staff
*Proposed.* People who sign in to the vendor's console and enable, disable, pin or move a brand's modules; they see no player of any brand.

### vendor's console
*Proposed.* ankka-vendor's backoffice for eitheror staff: every brand's modules from the mirrored module entities.

### entitlement
*Proposed.* The vendor's record that a brand may run a module on a channel or at a pinned version, with its terms, start and end.

### agent
*Proposed.* The vendor's service in a brand's project: holds the module entity, applies and removes modules through the brand's own control plane.

### module entity
*Proposed.* The agent's record of what is enabled, running and failed in a brand: module, version, channel, each service's state, the time of the last change, and any missing configuration key.

### channel
*Proposed.* A name at the vendor pointing at one release of one module; a brand's entitlement follows a channel unless pinned.

### module manifest
*Proposed.* The one description of a module, `modules/<module>/module.json`: services, grants, topics, dependencies, configuration keys, retention and any condition on ending.
Avoid: manifest file

### configuration key
*Proposed.* A name a module manifest declares for a value the brand supplies; the manifest never carries the value.

### values file
*Proposed.* What one brand supplies, `brands/<slug>/values.json`: realm settings, real money, secret names, hostnames and a value for every configuration key its modules name; never a version, service or grant.
Avoid: brand manifest

### modules tool
*Proposed.* `tools/modules`, reached as `ankka casino`: validates a module manifest or a values file, renders what the agent would apply, applies in local mode, reads status.

### retention
*Proposed.* How long a disabled module's data is kept, as its manifest declares; re-enabling within it attaches to the kept data.

### release job
*Proposed.* The job a tag runs: builds every module's images and publishes every manifest to the vendor at the tag's version, moving no channel.

### modules page
*Proposed.* The backoffice page where a brand operator sees their brand's modules; it offers no enable.

### platform modules topic
*Proposed.* `platform.modules`: one fact per change to a brand's module entity.

### market route
*Proposed.* A route the predictions module serves.

### game route
*Proposed.* A route the casino module serves.

## Predictions

### market
*Proposed.* A question with two or more outcomes, one of which will be resolved true; drafted, approved, opened, closed, resolved or voided, then final. The rules are the markets library's (ankka-predictions).

### outcome
*Proposed.* One answer to a market's question, with a book of its own.

### draft market
*Proposed.* A market an operator has written and not yet approved; not shown to players.

### approved market
*Proposed.* A market approved and not yet open; not shown to players.

### open market
*Proposed.* A market players may trade in.

### final
*Proposed.* Of a market: resolved or void and past its review window; its positions are paid.

### void
*Proposed.* Of a market: cancelled after opening; each share is paid its equal part.

### markets permission
*Proposed.* The permission on the brand_<slug> role that lets an operator draft, approve, close, resolve and void markets.

### share
*Proposed.* One unit of a position in an outcome; pays one unit if the market resolves to that outcome.

### order
*Proposed.* A player's instruction to buy or sell shares of an outcome at a price; checked, funded and then resting in the book.

### buy order
*Proposed.* An order to buy shares; its cost is reserved before it rests.

### sell order
*Proposed.* An order to sell shares the player holds; reserves nothing.

### book
*Proposed.* The resting orders of one outcome, matched by price then time; the exchange library's.

### trade
*Proposed.* A match between a buy order and a sell order at a price for a quantity; settled through the wallet under its id.

### position
*Proposed.* The shares a player holds in an outcome of a market.

### price
*Proposed.* What a share costs, from 1 to 99 minor units.

### last price
*Proposed.* The price of an outcome's most recent trade.

### best bid
*Proposed.* The highest resting buy price in an outcome's book.

### best ask
*Proposed.* The lowest resting sell price in an outcome's book.

### depth
*Proposed.* The quantity resting at each price in a book.

### market catalogue
*Proposed.* The open markets a player may read, with each outcome's prices and depth.

### bet gate
*Proposed.* responsible-gambling's may-bet, asked with the order's cost before an order is funded.

### exchange
*Proposed.* The predictions module's service that holds the books and implements the gate and ledger ports.

### markets service
*Proposed.* The predictions module's service that holds markets and implements the resolution port.

### reasoning service
*Proposed.* A reasoning graph a brand's values may name; when named, approved markets are mirrored to it and prices observed.

### question
*Proposed.* What a market is mirrored to the reasoning service as.

### hypothesis
*Proposed.* What an outcome is mirrored to the reasoning service as, one per outcome.

### price observation
*Proposed.* An outcome's last price written to the reasoning service, at most once a minute per outcome.

### exchange orders topic
*Proposed.* `exchange.orders`: one fact per order change, keyed by the player.

### exchange trades topic
*Proposed.* `exchange.trades`: one fact per trade, keyed by the market.

### exchange prices topic
*Proposed.* `exchange.prices`: one fact per change of an outcome's last price, keyed by the market.

### markets topic
*Proposed.* `markets.events`: one fact per change to a market, keyed by the market.

### platform audit topic
*Proposed.* `platform.audit`, as spec 001 names it.

## Reservations and accounts

### reservation
*Proposed.* Money set aside from real balance under an order's key; released in part or whole, or drawn down by a settlement.

### reserved amount
*Proposed.* The sum of a wallet's reservations.

### available amount
*Proposed.* Real balance less the reserved amount; what a bet or a hold may take.

### settlement
*Proposed.* One keyed move of a trade's cost from the buyer's reservation to the seller's real balance, across two wallets.

### non-player account
*Proposed.* A ledger in the wallet service owned by a service principal and keyed by what it is for: a pool account or a clearing account.

### pool account
*Proposed.* The non-player account of one market that backs its complete sets; never below zero.

### clearing account
*Proposed.* The non-player account a pool owns in a brand's wallet service; may go below zero to its credit line.

### credit line
*Proposed.* How far below zero a clearing account may go, set by the pool's entitlement.

### resolution payment
*Proposed.* A credit to a player's real balance from a market's pool account when the market is final, keyed by the market and the position. Not a payout, which is an affiliate's.

### service principal
*Proposed.* A service, named as its project and name, that owns a non-player account or calls a granted route.
